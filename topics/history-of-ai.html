History of Artificial Intelligence

⏱ 7 min read

The history of AI spans over 70 years — from theoretical foundations in logic and mathematics to today's large language models. Understanding this timeline helps appreciate both the progress made and the challenges that remain.

The AI Timeline

1943
McCulloch & Pitts — First Neural Network Model
Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts published a mathematical model of a neuron, laying the groundwork for artificial neural networks.
1950
Alan Turing — "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"
Turing proposed the famous "Turing Test" — a method to determine if a machine can exhibit intelligent behaviour indistinguishable from a human. He asked: "Can machines think?"
1956
Dartmouth Conference — Birth of AI
John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Claude Shannon, and others organised the Dartmouth Summer Research Project. McCarthy coined the term "Artificial Intelligence" here. This is officially the birth of AI as a discipline.
1957–1974
The Golden Age of AI
Early optimism. Programs like Logic Theorist, ELIZA (first chatbot), and SHRDLU showed machines could solve algebraic problems and understand simple English. Heavy government funding in USA and UK.
1974–1980
First AI Winter
Progress stalled. Computers lacked speed and memory. Tasks like language translation proved far harder than expected. The Lighthill Report (UK) and US government funding cuts led to the first "AI Winter."
1980–1987
Expert Systems Revival
Rule-based "expert systems" like XCON (DEC) brought AI to industry. Japan's Fifth Generation Computer project injected billions into AI research. The field revived commercially.
1987–1993
Second AI Winter
Expert systems proved brittle and expensive to maintain. The Lisp Machine market collapsed. DARPA cut funding again. A second winter followed.
1997
Deep Blue Defeats Kasparov
IBM's Deep Blue became the first computer to defeat a reigning world chess champion (Garry Kasparov) under tournament conditions. A landmark moment for narrow AI.
2006
Deep Learning Revolution Begins
Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, and Yoshua Bengio demonstrated that deep neural networks could be trained effectively using improved techniques. The term "deep learning" was popularised.
2012
AlexNet — ImageNet Breakthrough
AlexNet, a deep CNN by Hinton's team, won the ImageNet competition by a massive margin. This proved deep learning was vastly superior for image recognition, triggering a global AI gold rush.
2016–2017
AlphaGo & Transformer Architecture
Google DeepMind's AlphaGo defeated world Go champion Lee Sedol — considered impossible at the time. Google also published "Attention Is All You Need", introducing the Transformer architecture that powers modern LLMs.
2020–Present
Large Language Models Era
GPT-3 (2020), DALL-E, ChatGPT (2022), GPT-4 (2023), Gemini, Claude, and others transformed how the world interacts with AI. Generative AI became mainstream.

Key Figures in AI History

  • Alan Turing — theoretical foundations, Turing Test
  • John McCarthy — coined "AI", created Lisp programming language
  • Marvin Minsky — co-founder of MIT AI Lab, neural network pioneer
  • Geoffrey Hinton — "Godfather of Deep Learning", backpropagation
  • Yann LeCun — convolutional neural networks, computer vision
  • Yoshua Bengio — deep learning theory, sequence models
  • Andrej Karpathy — computer vision, modern LLM training

Why AI Winters Happened

⚠️
Lesson from History
AI Winters occurred because of overpromising (hype cycles), insufficient compute, lack of data, and brittle systems. Today's AI renaissance is more sustainable — backed by real commercial value, massive data, and cloud-scale compute.
